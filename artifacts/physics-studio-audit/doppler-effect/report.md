Physics conventions: air is at rest with c=343 m/s. Source control v_s is positive right. Observer control v_o is positive left, toward the initial source; its global x velocity is −v_o. All internal distances/times are metres/seconds. Source x_s(t)=−.8+v_s t and microphone x_o(t)=3.8−v_o t. Speeds are subsonic within ±50 m/s.

For rightward propagation, f_+=f(c+v_o)/(c−v_s). For leftward propagation, f_−=f(c−v_o)/(c+v_s). Front/rear are fixed +x/−x directions, not permanent synonyms for approaching/receding or high/low frequency. This avoids misleading labels under negative velocities. Wavelengths in the medium are λ_+=(c−v_s)/f and λ_−=(c+v_s)/f. Observer motion affects arrival frequency, not spatial wavelength. The former page used the same observer sign for both received frequencies and incorrectly set wavelength=c/f_observed for a moving observer; both errors are fixed.

Each crest was emitted at t_e=(k+1/4)/f. Its centre remains x_e=−.8+v_s t_e, and its radius grows as c(t−t_e). Adjacent forward/rear intersections therefore have the calculated spatial spacings. Source uniform motion/emission before t=0 provides the preexisting fronts visible at reset. An independent browser check reads actual rendered ellipse centres/radii and native source/microphone object positions, not only labels. Wavefront colors identify direction; opacity is a display cue, not a sound-level prediction.

The actual microphone uses τ=|x_o(t)−x_s(t)|/(c−direction*v_s) and t_e=t−τ. This satisfies c(t−t_e)=|x_o(t)−x_s(t_e)|. Received unit pressure is sin(2πft_e), and differentiating t_e gives the appropriate front/rear received frequency. It switches after the source passes the microphone.
