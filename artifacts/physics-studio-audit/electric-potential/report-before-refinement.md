## Physical model

V(r) = k sum(q_i / |r-r_i|) - Vref; E(r) = k sum(q_i (r-r_i) / |r-r_i|^3); |gradient V| = |E|; U = qtest V. k = 8.99e9 SI and qtest = +1 microcoulomb. The three default fixed sources are +5 microcoulombs at (-1,0,0) m, -5 at (0,-1,0), +3 at (1.2,0.8,0). Default probe is (0,0,0.5) m. Correct outputs are V = 17668.6344627 V, U = 0.0176686344627 J, and |gradient V| = 44809.1251349 V/m. The mockup's 12.4 V and other readings are inconsistent with its stated source magnitudes and distances and are not reproduced as fake results.

Potential, mesh colors and base contours describe the physical z slice selected by the height control. Vertical plot ordinate is V, not spatial z. Surface height/color are clipped at +/-50 kV for readability; measurements use the unsoftened point law. Grid uses 80 x 80 cells / 12800 triangles by default and marching triangles with 5 kV contour spacing. At z=0 a sample exactly at a source is excluded; the charge marker indicates the appropriate signed plot limit. Charge glyphs indicate source x/y positions on the potential plot, not physical sphere conductors. The numerical singularity exclusion is r < 1e-12 m.

The reference selector switches from zero at infinity to zero at physical point (0,0,3 m); a spatially constant shift changes V and U and preserves E. Three distinct layouts are supported. A scalar cancellation can have V=0 and nonzero electric field. Playback scans the externally controlled probe along x at 0.2 m per simulation second, reflecting at +/-2 m; this is a measurement scan, not a test charge obeying a free-particle equation. Sources remain fixed; no backreaction is simulated.

Scientific references: [OpenStax, calculations of electric potential](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/7-3-calculations-of-electric-potential), [field from potential](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/7-4-determining-field-from-potential), and [potential difference](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/7-2-electric-potential-and-potential-difference).
