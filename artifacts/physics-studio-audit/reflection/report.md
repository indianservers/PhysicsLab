Model configuration angles use the instrument's degrees; trigonometry converts them to radians. All lengths/time are metres/seconds. With mirror rotation β, tangent t=(cosβ,sinβ), normal n=(sinβ,−cosβ) in the horizontal x,z plane. Incidence θ is relative to the current normal. Incoming travel direction d=t sinθ−n cosθ. Reflected travel direction r=d−2(d·n)n. Both remain unit vectors; tangential component is preserved and normal component reversed.

Mirror Rotation reconfigures the source and receiver with the mirror while holding the chosen relative incidence. It is not a fixed-incoming-source experiment. Ray Count creates 1–5 parallel aperture rays at 4 mm spacing. Every ray is independently intersected with the mirror plane, then intersected with a common receiver plane.
